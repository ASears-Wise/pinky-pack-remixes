The story

The ear that
built an era.

Before an algorithm decided what was a hit, there was the record pool — and Ray Pinky Velasquez was in the room.

In 1970s Manhattan the record pool was the filter. Labels pressed the promos, DJs pulled them by the armful, and what came back decided what went to radio. Ray was pulling a hundred records a week — listening hard, marking them up, sending the verdict back to the labels.

He spun at the Ipanema Discotheque, two blocks from Studio 54. He ran A&R at Vanguard Records from 1979 to 1984, signed Spectrum City and produced their first single — before they became Public Enemy. Today he is a co-founder of Legends of Vinyl, and through Pinky Pack Productions he brings that ear to your record.
